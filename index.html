  • One fact, shown two ways. For each audience, Google’s API sends a number (0) and a size bucket (Under 500). Google Ads shows 0, as reported by the customer. The Factors app shows the bucket.
  • Why the number is 0. Google shows a number only once a list has 100 members. Below that it shows 0. The “500” in “Under 500” is only the top of the lowest bucket, not a minimum. Our estimate is 13 and 66 matched people on the two audiences that hold people and report a match rate (the other five that hold people report none yet), both under the 100 line. A 0 does not mean nobody matched.
  • Why the number is small. Few visitor IDs reach Google: on this site the cookie banner denies ad consent by default and, in a clean-browser test on the home page, its script removed the Factors cookie about 85 ms after it was set. And four of the eleven audiences are empty right now. They are built on the “Hot” level, which needs 60 points, and the highest score any account has today is 30. Audience 2 also accepts “Warm”. One account is Warm (score 30), but it has no country or industry in Factors’ company data, and Audience 2 also requires Canada or the United States and one of 11 listed industries, so it does not qualify.

How it works — and where it drops off

Each night Factors uploads visitor IDs to a Google Ads audience. By design, Google can match an ID only if the same ID reached Google earlier through the site’s Google tag. Google’s public pages do not spell out the matching.

        What can be done

        The target is about 100 matched active people in one list. 500 is not the target. Effort and impact below are our judgement, not measurements.

        From Google’s documentation: a list shows a number from 100 members, and can serve from 100 active matched users in the last 30 days (for lists uploaded after 1 Feb 2024). The two audiences we can estimate hold about 13 and 66 matched people.

        Why four audiences are empty

        A settings effect, not an outage. Audiences 1, 2, 3 and 5 are built on the engagement level “Hot”. Hot needs 60 points, and the highest score any account has today is 30. Audience 2 also accepts “Warm”. One account is Warm (score 30), but it has no country or industry in Factors’ company data, and Audience 2 also requires Canada or the United States and one of 11 listed industries, so it does not qualify. So these segments match nobody, and the nightly sync removed everyone from the four Google lists.

        What happened

        The account-scoring rules were replaced on 2 Oct, and the level thresholds changed from percentile-based to fixed scores. The four audiences emptied over the days after the change; the last members were removed on 13–14 Oct.

        What did not change. The other 7 audiences do not use engagement level and are populated normally. No segment was edited or deleted. The sync is healthy (no errors) and the scoring job ran on 15 Oct.
        Likely fix (untested). Set the Warm and Hot thresholds to scores that accounts actually reach under the new rules, or go back to percentile-based thresholds. Hot accounts should then reappear after the next scoring run (every 3–5 days), and the audiences refill at the nightly sync after that, for accounts that also match each audience’s region and industry filters.

        What Google’s documentation says

        “For privacy purposes, the user list size shows as zero until the list has at least 100 members. After that, the size is rounded to the two most significant digits.”
        Google Ads API, Customer Match guide
        • Serving minimum. Search, Display and YouTube each need at least 100 active users in the last 30 days. Customer lists have the same eligibility. The 100 applies to lists uploaded or refreshed after 1 Feb 2024; older lists still needed 1,000 matched users. Google recommends uploading 5,000+ members for the best chance of enough matches.
        • Buckets. LESS_THAN_FIVE_HUNDRED means “under 500 users”. The next is 500 to 1,000, then 1,000 to 10,000, and so on. There is no “under 100” bucket.
        • Screen against API. The API guide says a list “could appear smaller than expected” in the Google Ads Audience Manager, because that view shows the number of active users. Google does not document the exact label its screen shows for tiny lists.
        • Timing. List population can take up to 24 hours (API guide); the Help Center says matching can take up to 48 hours. These audiences are refreshed daily and the API returns definite values, so timing is not the cause here.
        • Matching. Contact lists match hashed email or phone against Google accounts; Workspace and corporate accounts can’t be targeted. For ID-based lists, Google’s public pages describe the ID as advertiser-assigned. By design, Google can match an ID only if the same ID reached Google earlier through the site’s Google tag. Google’s public pages do not spell out the matching.

        Glossary
        Audience
        A list of people that Factors sends to Google Ads, built from a segment.
        Segment
        The rule in Factors that picks which accounts belong in an audience.
        Matched audience size
        How many of the uploaded people Google could match to a Google user. Google hides small numbers.
        Size bucket / range
        Google’s coarse label for the size: “less than 500”, “500 to 1,000”, “1,000 to 10,000” and so on.
        Match rate
        The share of uploaded IDs that Google matched.
        ID-based (CRM_ID) list
        We upload a Factors visitor ID, not an email. By design, Google can match an ID only if the same ID reached Google earlier through the site’s Google tag. Google’s public pages do not spell out the matching.
        Consent banner / Consent Mode
        The website’s cookie banner and Google’s consent signals. When consent is denied, Google ad features may not use the visitor’s data.
        Active users
        Matched people who were active in the last 30 days. Google’s serving minimum counts these.
